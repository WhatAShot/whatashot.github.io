Electrocardio Panorama

A research series on continuous-view ECG reconstruction, with two complementary data resources: the original Nef-Net release and Panobench from NEF-NET v2.

Original Release · Nef-Net

IJCAI 2021

ECG waveform boundaries, lead geometry, and an interactive annotation tool.

1,000 manual annotation files31,142 computer-assisted files6 waveform boundary types

The downloadable Tianchi annotations mark the onset and offset of the P wave, QRS complex, and T wave as sample indices in JSON files. These boundaries define six intervals: P wave, PR segment, QRS complex, ST segment, T wave, and TP segment. The data loader extracts a cardiac cycle from one P-wave onset to the next.

The annotation tool is a Python desktop application built with PyQt5 and pyqtgraph. It displays leads II, V2, and V4 together; annotators position the cursor and press keys 1–6 to mark waveform boundaries, then save them as JSON. Example files use P on/off, R on/off, and T on/off; R on/off denotes the QRS boundaries.

The release also provides a shared mapping from the 12 standard leads to viewing angles (θ, φ), based on average measurements from 30 body CT scans. Combined with waveform boundaries, it supports both spatially conditioned ECG synthesis and temporally structured heartbeat modeling.

Archive counts refer to JSON files, not unique participants or heartbeats, and do not imply that every computer-assisted annotation was manually reviewed. The paper describes annotations for Tianchi and PTB; the current repository provides the two Tianchi archives linked below. Original ECG recordings come from the source datasets. Lead angles are shared reference geometry, not individual electrode-position measurements.

Panobench · NEF-NET v2

ICLR 2026

Dense ECG viewpoints with explicit spatial geometry.

48 views4,470 recordings10 seconds eachCT-derived angles

Six limb leads and 42 precordial leads provide much denser spatial coverage than a standard 12-lead ECG. Each viewpoint is annotated with CT-derived spherical coordinates (θ, φ), linking the recorded waveform to its observation direction.

This pairing makes Panobench a benchmark for geometry-aware ECG modeling, sparse-to-dense reconstruction, and synthesis at held-out viewpoints: predictions can be compared with recorded signals at the target views.
